'''Suzie McConnell-Serio''' (born July 29, 1966)<ref name="NCAA Coaches"/> is an American former [[women's basketball]] coach and player. She was the head coach for the women's basketball team at [[Duquesne University]] from 2007 to 2013 and then the [[University of Pittsburgh]] from 2013 to 2018. In 2004, she was named [[WNBA Coach of the Year]] as coach of the [[Minnesota Lynx]]. She was inducted into the [[Women's Basketball Hall of Fame]] in 2008.

==Career==
Born in [[Pittsburgh]], McConnell-Serio attended [[Seton-La Salle Catholic High School]], leading the Lady Rebels to the [[Pennsylvania Interscholastic Athletic Association|PIAA]] state championship in 1984. McConnell-Serio played college basketball at [[Penn State]]. In four seasons at Penn State (1984–85 through 1987–88), she helped the [[Penn State Lady Lions basketball|Lady Lions]] to a 95–33 record and four consecutive NCAA tournament appearances. In the process, she was named the school's first First-Team All-American while setting NCAA Division I records for career assists (1,307), assists in a season (355 in 1987) and season assist average (11.8 in 1987). Her assist total and assist per game average led the nation in both 1986 & 1987.<ref>{{cite web|title=Suzie McConnell-Serio|url=http://www.goduquesne.com/sports/w-baskbl/mtt/mcconnellserio_suzie00.html|access-date=29 March 2011}}</ref> She also set the record for most career points by anyone with 1,000-plus assists at 1,897 points. In 2011, [[Courtney Vandersloot]] of [[Gonzaga Bulldogs women's basketball|Gonzaga University]] passed her in number of assists in a single season with 367 in 2011, and number of career points with 1,000-plus assists with 2,073 points.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://stats.ncaa.org/player?game_sport_year_ctl_id=10420&stats_player_seq=849777 |title=2010–11 Women's Basketball Individual Statistics: Courtney Vandersloot |publisher=NCAA |access-date=April 3, 2011}}</ref> As of the start of the 2017–18 season, she still holds the records for most career assists, most assists per game in a season (and most assists per game in a season by a sophomore and by a junior), and most career [[triple-doubles]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/stats/w_basketball_RB/2018/D1.pdf |title=Division 1 Women's Basketball Records}}</ref> She graduated from Penn State in 1988. She won the [[Frances Pomeroy Naismith Award]] in 1988.<ref name="Frances Pomeroy Naismith"/> At the time, this award was given to the best women's basketball player in the country no taller than {{Height|ft=5|in=6}}; the height limit was later increased to {{Height|ft=5|in=8}} before it was discontinued in 2013. She also ended her college career with seven career triple-doubles, which at the time was an NCAA all-divisions women's record (later equaled by Louella Tomlinson of [[Saint Mary's Gaels women's basketball|Saint Mary's]]). This record has since been broken by [[Oregon Ducks women's basketball|Oregon]]'s [[Sabrina Ionescu]].<ref>{{cite news |date=December 31, 2017 |title=Ionescu sets NCAA mark for triple-doubles in Oregon's win |url=https://www.espn.com/womens-college-basketball/recap?gameId=400994440 |access-date=July 11, 2024 |website=ESPN.com |agency=Associated Press}}</ref>

Before joining the Minnesota Lynx, McConnell-Serio served as head coach of the girls' basketball team at [[Oakland Catholic High School]] in Pittsburgh for 13 years, from 1991 to 2003. During that time, her teams won 3 PIAA state championships (1993, 2001, 2003), were runners-up twice (2000–2002), and won five consecutive district championships (1999–2003). On December 17, 2002, she reached a coaching milestone with her 300th win. She finished her high school coaching career after the 2002–03 season with a 321–86 record.

McConnell-Serio also played in the [[WNBA]]. The former [[point guard]] played three seasons with the [[Cleveland Rockers]] (1998–2000). As a first-year player in 1998, she earned WNBA Newcomer of the Year and All-WNBA First Team honors after averaging 8.6 ppg and 6.4 apg (second in the league). She was a two-time winner of the [[Kim Perrot Sportsmanship Award]] (1998 and 2000) and finished her professional career with averages of 6.4 ppg and 4.6 apg in 81 contests.

In January 2003, McConnell-Serio returned to the WNBA as head coach of the [[Minnesota Lynx]] and took a team that finished 10–22 in the previous season to an 18–16 record and first-ever playoff appearance.

One year later, McConnell-Serio took a team picked to finish last in the Western Conference by a number of preseason publications and made a return trip to the playoffs and in the process was named the 2004 [[WNBA Coach of the Year Award|WNBA Coach of the Year]]. The '04 Lynx, who earned a franchise-best #3 seed in the WNBA Western Conference, tied franchise records for wins (18), home victories (11) and road wins (7) all set in McConnell-Serio's first season. In addition, Minnesota enjoyed a franchise-record six-game winning streak.

However, the Lynx struggled in the 2005 and 2006 season, and McConnell-Serio resigned at mid-season on July 23, 2006.

Prior to her professional playing career, McConnell-Serio won two [[Olympic Games|Olympic]] medals. She was a member of a [[gold medal]]-winning U.S. women's basketball team in the [[1988 Summer Olympics]], and earned a [[bronze medal]] at the [[1992 Summer Olympics]].

McConnell-Serio also won a [[gold medal]] at the 1991 [[World University Games]]. She won the 2004 [[WNBA Coach of the Year Award]]. In 1999, ''[[Sports Illustrated]]'' magazine named her one of the Top 50 Athletes of the Century in [[Pennsylvania]].

On April 13, 2007, McConnell-Serio was named the head coach at [[Duquesne University]].

On April 12, 2013, McConnell-Serio was named the head coach at the [[Pittsburgh Panthers women's basketball|University of Pittsburgh]]. She was fired on April 5, 2018.<ref>{{cite news |last=Batko |first=Brian |title=Suzie McConnell-Serio out as Pitt women's basketball coach |url=https://www.post-gazette.com/sports/Pitt/2018/04/05/Suzie-McConnell-Serio-fired-pitt-women-s-basketball-heather-lyke-ad-pittsburgh-panthers/stories/201804050137 |newspaper=Pittsburgh Post-Gazette |date=April 5, 2018}}</ref>

==Personal life==
McConnell-Serio is married to Pete Serio, and they have four children, Peter, Jordan, Mandi, and Madison, who all have played basketball.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Suzie McConnell-Serio - Women's Basketball Coach|url=https://pittsburghpanthers.com/sports/womens-basketball/roster/coaches/suzie-mcconnell-serio/15|access-date=2021-05-30|website=Pitt Panthers #H2P|language=en}}</ref>

McConnell-Serio is a member of the [[Women's Basketball Hall of Fame]]. She was inducted into it on June 14, 2008, at the [[Bijou Theatre (Knoxville, Tennessee)|Bijou Theatre]] in [[Knoxville, Tennessee]].<ref name="wbhof"/>

McConnell-Serio's brother, Tom, played two seasons at [[Davidson College]] and was the head coach for [[Saint Francis Red Flash men's basketball|Saint Francis (Pennsylvania)]] from 1992 to 1999 and has been an assistant coach at [[Wake Forest Demon Deacons men's basketball|Wake Forest]], [[Marquette Golden Eagles men's basketball|Marquette]], and [[Dayton Flyers men's basketball|Dayton]] as well as women's assistant coach at [[Colorado Buffaloes women's basketball|Colorado]] with their sister, Kathy. Before becoming the current head coach of the [[Indiana University of Pennsylvania]] women's team, he served as an assistant at Old Dominion.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Tom McConnell - Women's Basketball Coach|url=https://iupathletics.com/sports/womens-basketball/roster/coaches/tom-mcconnell/798|access-date=2021-05-30|website=Indiana University of Pennsylvania Athletics|language=en}}</ref>

McConnell-Serio's sister, Kathy, who was her teammate on the 1984 PIAA championship team, served as her associate head coach at the University of Pittsburgh. She was previously the head coach at the universities of [[Tulsa Golden Hurricane women's basketball|Tulsa]] (1999–2005) and [[Colorado Buffaloes women's basketball|Colorado]] (2006–10) before accepting a position as an assistant coach with the WNBA's [[Tulsa Shock]]. She was also an assistant coach at the [[Illinois Fighting Illini women's basketball|University of Illinois]] (1996–99) and [[Rutgers Scarlet Knights women's basketball|Rutgers]] (1994–95). Her first coaching job out of college was at the University of Pittsburgh as a recruiting coordinator during the 1991 season. She played in four NCAA championships while at the [[Virginia Cavaliers women's basketball|University of Virginia]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Kathy McConnell-Miller - Women's Basketball Coach|url=https://pittsburghpanthers.com/sports/womens-basketball/roster/coaches/kathy-mcconnell-miller/16|access-date=2021-05-30|website=Pitt Panthers #H2P|language=en}}</ref>

McConnell-Serio's brother, Tim, played at [[Waynesburg University]] and has served as the boys' coach at Chartiers Valley from 1992 to 2018 and has won over 80 percent of his games while earning his 400th career win in 2010. In 2018, he moved to coach the Chartiers Valley girls' basketball team.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Tim McConnell - Coach/Basketball - Western Chapter of PA Sports Hall of Fame|url=http://westernpasportshof.org/tim-mcconnell-coach-basketball/|access-date=2021-05-30|website=Robert "Tick" Cloherty Western Chapter of the Pennsylvania Sports Hall of Fame|language=en-US}}</ref>

McConnell-Serio's sister, Maureen, played at the University of Pittsburgh, and her nephew, [[T. J. McConnell|T. J.]] (Tim's son) was on the Duquesne men's basketball team for two seasons (2011 and 2012) before transferring to [[Arizona Wildcats men's basketball|Arizona]]. He currently plays for the [[Indiana Pacers]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=T.J. McConnell Stats, News, Bio |url=https://www.espn.com/nba/player/_/id/2530530 |access-date=2021-05-30 |website=ESPN |language=en}}</ref> Her niece, [[Megan McConnell|Megan]], played on the Duquesne women's basketball team and now plays for the [[Phoenix Mercury]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Stockey |first=Andrew |title=Megan McConnell dominates Atlantic 10 basketball as family legacy thrives in Pittsburgh |website=[[WTAE-TV]] |url=https://www.wtae.com/article/megan-mcconnell-duquesne-dukes-basketball/63475620 |date=January 21, 2025 |access-date=June 8, 2025}}</ref>
