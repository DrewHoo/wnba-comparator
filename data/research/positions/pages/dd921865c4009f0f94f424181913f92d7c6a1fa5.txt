'''Angela Deanne Bjorklund''' (born July 14, 1989) is an American former [[NCAA]] [[basketball player]] for the [[Tennessee Lady Vols]]. She played professionally for the [[Chicago Sky]], who drafted her in the [[2011 WNBA draft]]. Her older sister, Jami, was a [[Forward (basketball)|forward]] for [[Kelly Graves]]' [[Gonzaga Bulldogs]] women's basketball team from 2005 to 2009, playing alongside Sky teammate at the time; [[Courtney Vandersloot]].

== High school career ==
Bjorklund attended [[University High School (Washington)|University High School]] in [[Spokane Valley, Washington]].

Bjorklund was named a [[Women's Basketball Coaches Association|WBCA]] All-American. She participated in the 2007 WBCA High School All-America Game, where she scored eleven points.<ref name="WBCA Box Scores" />

==USA Basketball==
In 2007, Bjorklund was a member of the [[USA Women's U18 and U19 teams|USA Women's U19]] team which won the gold medal at the FIBA U19 World Championship in [[Bratislava, Slovakia]]. The event was held in July and August 2007, when the USA team defeated Sweden to win the championship. Bjorklund helped the team the gold medal, scoring 7.4 points per game.<ref name="2007 U19"/><ref>{{Cite web|url=http://sports.aol.com/ncaawbb-players/angie-bjorklund/23267|title = Sports News & latest headlines from AOL}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=http://girlshoops.scout.com/a.z?s=209&p=8&c=1&nid=1208926|title=NFL, College Sports, NBA and Recruiting}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.utladyvols.com/sports/w-baskbl/mtt/bjorklund_angie01.html |title=Player Bio: Angie Bjorklund University of Tennessee Athletics |access-date=December 1, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110717033545/http://www.utladyvols.com/sports/w-baskbl/mtt/bjorklund_angie01.html |archive-date=July 17, 2011 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==College career==
Bjorklund became the 10th [[Tennessee Lady Volunteers basketball|Lady Vol]] freshman to start their basketball career in the starting lineup.<ref name="Angie Bjorklund">{{cite web |date=April 11, 2015 |title=Angie Bjorklund |url=http://www.utsports.com/sports/w-baskbl/mtt/bjorklund_angie01.html |url-status=dead |accessdate=April 11, 2015 |work=UTSports.com}}</ref> She played all 38 games during her freshman season, where Tennessee won the National Championship against Stanford by a final score of 64–48, earning [[Pat Summitt]] her eighth and eventually, final National Championship as head coach.<ref name="Angie Bjorklund"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://sports.espn.go.com/ncw/recap?gameId=284000063|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080411230710/http://sports.espn.go.com/ncw/recap?gameId=284000063|url-status=dead|archive-date=April 11, 2008|title=Parker, Tennessee earn back-to-back titles with rout of Stanford|date=April 10, 2008|work=Sports.ESPN.go.com|accessdate=April 11, 2015}}</ref> Bjorklund finished her college career with 300 3-pointers, becoming the Lady Vols all-time 3 points record, which was previously held by [[Shanna Zolman]], who had 266 3-pointers in her career.<ref name="Angie Bjorklund"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.spokesman.com/stories/2010/dec/23/bjorklund-ties-tennessee-career-record/|title=Bjorklund ties Tennessee career record for three pointers|date=December 23, 2010|work=spokesman.com|accessdate=April 11, 2015}}</ref>

In 2011, she became the 29th Lady Vol ever selected in the WNBA draft.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Stipe |first=Zach |date=2011-04-11 |title=Chicago Chooses Bjorklund in WNBA Draft |url=https://utsports.com/news/2011/4/11/Chicago_Chooses_Bjorklund_in_WNBA_Draft |access-date=2025-09-13 |website=University of Tennessee Athletics |language=en}}</ref>

==Post basketball career==
Bjorklund was hired as the [[Santa Clara University]] Director of Women's Basketball Operations position prior to the 2013–14 season under [[Jennifer Mountain]], who was assistant coach during her sister Jami's freshman, sophomore, and junior years at Gonzaga.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://santaclarabroncos.com/sports/w-baskbl/coaches/bjorklund_angie|title = Santa Clara}}</ref> She has also been a graduate assistant coach and a sport psychologist at the [[University of Idaho]].

In 2025, she was inducted into the Hooptown USA Hall of Fame.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lee |first=Greg |date=2025-06-24 |title='I miss playing': Hooptown Hall of Famer Angie Bjorklund, among the best ever from Spokane, adjusts to life as a coach |url=https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2025/jun/24/i-miss-playing-hooptown-hall-of-famer-angie-bjorkl/ |access-date=2025-09-13 |website=The Spokesman-Review |language=en}}</ref>

== Personal life ==
Bjorklund comes from a family of athletics.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Valhovich |first=Mike |date=2005-02-17 |title=Basketball through and through |url=https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2005/feb/17/basketball-through-and-through/ |access-date=2025-09-13 |website=The Spokesman-Review |language=en}}</ref> Her sister is [[Jami Bjorklund]], who also played high school and college basketball.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2007-12-12 |title=Battle of Bjorklund sisters highlights Gonzaga at No. 1 Tennessee game |url=https://www.espn.com/espn/wire?section=ncw&id=3152186 |access-date=2025-09-13 |website=ESPN.com |language=en}}</ref> Their Uncle Steve Ranniger played basketball at Oregon; grandfather Duane Ranniger played basketball at Washington State; and grandfather Leon Bjorklund ran track at Washington.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2007-12-16 |title=No. 1 Tennessee Too Much for Gonzaga |url=https://gozags.com/news/2007/12/16/No_1_Tennessee_Too_Much_for_Gonzaga.aspx |access-date=2025-09-13 |website=Gonzaga University Athletics |language=en}}</ref> Her parents are Jim and Kris Bjorklund.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Stalwick |first=Howie |date=2007-12-12 |title=A Family Affair |url=https://www.inlander.com/news/a-family-affair-2180566 |access-date=2025-09-13 |website=Inlander |language=en}}</ref> In addition to basketball, she also played soccer and volleyball throughout most of her childhood.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2011-05-19 |title=The Credit is Due: A Chat with Angie Bjorklund |url=https://www.wnba.com/archive/wnba/sky/news/the_credit_is_due__chat_with_2011_05_19.html |access-date=2025-09-13}}</ref>

She has a bachelor's degree in psychology from the University of Tennessee<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kittrell |first=Lauren |date=2011-01-19 |title=Bjorklund embracing senior year |url=https://www.utdailybeacon.com/sports/all/bjorklund-embracing-senior-year/article_4cbba48b-5cac-5676-84ad-0c41dabcfd46.html |access-date=2025-09-13 |website=The Daily Beacon |language=en}}</ref> and a Master's degree in sport psychology from the University of Idaho.

She currently lives in [[Spain]] with her partner [[Rosó Buch]], a Spanish basketball player.
